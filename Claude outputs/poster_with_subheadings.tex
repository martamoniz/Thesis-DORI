\begin{minipage}[t]{\colA}\topcol
  \begin{doriblock}[height=\rowonehgt]{INTRODUCTION}

Interventional radiology is the medical specialty subject to the highest
occupational exposure to ionising radiation [1]. Currently, occupational monitoring relies on
passive dosimeters, which report retrospective dose information at monthly
intervals at best. The ability to
anticipate and to act upon unnecessary exposure is therefore limited [2].
 
This work explores the development of DORI (\textbf{\textit{DO}}símetro para 
\textbf{\textit{R}}adiologia de \textbf{\textit{I}}ntervenção), a personal and active solution for real-time dose monitoring. DORI targets the detection
of scattered radiation through a low-cost unit that can be replicated for
monitoring at chest, neck, hand and eye level.

  \end{doriblock}

    \subhead{Hardware}

    equivalent properties, coupled to a silicon photomultiplier.

    \subhead{Energy Calibration}

    Bremsstrahlung endpoints acquired between 20 and 50\,kVp, combined with the
    $^{241}$Am photopeak at 59.5\,keV in a weighted least-squares fit. The
    $^{57}$Co Compton edge was used as an independent validation point.
